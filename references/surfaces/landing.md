# Surface: landing and marketing pages (Persuade)

The visitor decides in seconds whether this is for them. Design is the product here. The page earns attention, then action, with facts.

## The page's argument

Write it in one line before designing: *who arrives, what they must believe, what they do next.* Every section serves it or goes.

## The first screen

- **A headline that says what the product does for whom.** Two lines or fewer on desktop, three at most on a small phone (320 to 390 px). Specific, plain, in this product's own nouns. Shorten the words before you shrink the type.
- **One subhead** of at most about 25 words, in secondary colour, at most 36rem wide.
- **One primary action** and at most one secondary. Nothing stacked underneath.
- **One visual that shows something true**: the product itself, its output, or one concrete metaphor. Never filler.
- **A quiet navigation**: logo, the existing links, one button. On phones, one menu button.
- It composes as a whole at 1440 and 390: no dead band, the next section hinting below the fold.

## Hero compositions

- **Scene:** one image edge to edge, subject on one side, headline on the calm empty part, product view rising from the bottom edge.
- **Asymmetric split:** text one side, the visual given at least half the width, text centred vertically against it.
- **Text over product view:** headline, actions, then a large preview that runs past the fold.
- **Statement:** for a very short hero with no visual, large type alone, left-aligned.

## Sections

- 4 to 7 sections is plenty. Each has a different job and a different composition from its neighbours; none is a grid of identical cards.
- Common jobs: show the product working, explain how it fits the person's life, prove with specifics, answer objections, offer the next step.
- **Proof is specific and checkable**: real numbers with sources, named customers who agreed, product screenshots. If it cannot be verified, it does not go on the page.
- **Pricing** (if any): plain, comparable, the recommended plan quietly distinguished, no dark patterns.
- **The closing section** repeats the one action. The footer is a designed close, not a sitemap.

## Rhythm

- Space between sections 80 to 128; inside a section far smaller.
- Equal outer margins; a section never huddles on one side.
- Left-align long text. Centre at most one moment.

## Images and media

- Real beats generated beats stock beats none. Never a gradient blob, orb, robot or glowing brain.
- Crop, ratio and treatment are consistent. One dominant image beats six small ones.
- Every image has real alt text and a size set to avoid layout shift. Below-the-fold images load lazily.
- Credit found images where the licence asks.

## Copy

Plain and specific, no hype words, no unverifiable claims, no greeting. Buttons say what happens. Numbers carry their comparison.

## Motion and effects

At most one entrance and one or two purposeful moments (a product preview that animates its real function). Shader grounds and scroll scenes only for pages whose product is technical or visual, with static fallbacks on phones and with reduced motion.

## Performance and SEO basics

One `h1`, descriptive title and meta description, social preview image, performance budget (hero image under about 200 KB, LCP under 2.5 s on mid-range phones), real `<a>` links.

## Bright element

One visual that shows something true, or the headline set at scale.
