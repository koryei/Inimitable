# Round 7, 26 November 2026

The question: with the changes made after round 6 (a base starter with no layout, a layout and a material taken from the product's artifact, a frame check), **does the skilled page now beat a page built with no skill on distinctiveness?**

**Short answer: no. The bar was that skilled pages beat the baseline, and they did not.** They did better than in round 6 on some measures, they were still remembered less and judged more swappable, and all three judges grouped all four skilled pages as one hand.

A third judge saw the eight first screens with all text blurred: all eight products identified correctly; mean ownability 4.0 skilled against 3.25 baseline.

## One hand

All three judges, independently, put all four skilled pages in one group. What they named: the same neutral grotesque, small-radius buttons with a ↗ arrow, an outlined button at top right, headline on the left with copy and button to the right, thin rules, flat pale grounds, tiny mono "Example data" captions.

Most of that is the base starter and the motion kit: Geist for display and reading, `.m-arrow` on the primary button, the mono `.note`, a pale default ground in light theme. Removing the layout moved the sameness into the type and the controls.

`frame.mjs` passed all four (highest pair 0.69, below the 0.75 line; highest pair among the baselines 0.72). **It does not see what the judges saw.** It compares structure and a few style traits, not the typeface, the button's furniture or the caption habit.

## What the builders reported

- `--accent` wrote a derived colour instead of the one given (two builders). **Fixed after the round.**
- The base's `.first` rule overrode their layouts (two builders). **Fixed after the round.**
- `check.mjs` reported false contrast on text in a label with a custom checkbox. Not fixed.
- `signs.mjs` "Gradient logo tile" fires on small patterned data keys, and `score.mjs` cannot allow it. Not fixed.
- `check.mjs` and `craft.mjs` only see the loaded state; `craft.mjs` skips visually hidden radio inputs. Not fixed.
- `shoot.mjs --full` caught a headline mid-entrance in one run. Not fixed.

## Limits

- **Judge noise is large.** The three reused baseline pages scored 4.5 mean ownability from round 6's judges and 3.33 from this round's. So the change in ownability between rounds (3.0 to 3.67 on the same three briefs) is smaller than the change between judges, and is not evidence of improvement. Within this round, with the same judges, the arms tie.
- Same model family builds and judges. Two judges, plus one on blurred screens. No real users.
- One build per arm per brief.
- The changes were designed from round 6's verdicts on briefs 12 to 14. Brief 15 was held out, and there the skilled page lost.
- The frame check's weights and threshold were set against the round 6 pages.
- Each page was combined into one image this round; round 6 judges saw separate screenshots.

## Next steps (proposals, not done)

1. **Make type and controls part of the concept.** The base should not decide the typeface, the button arrow or the caption style. That needs more than one display face to choose from, which means adding licensed font files (a decision for the owner).
2. **Strip the base further:** no `.m-arrow` and no mono captions by default.
3. **Teach the frame check what the judges saw:** typeface family name, button furniture, ground lightness. Treat it as a smoke alarm, not a verdict.
4. **Study the wins.** The two baselines remembered most and brief 13's skilled page each commit to one physical idea from the product all the way through. "Mark an element `data-device`" is weaker than that.
5. **Next round:** new briefs only, three builds per arm, and a judge from a different model.
