# Round 6, 25 November 2026

The question this round asks is the one the owner keeps asking: **is the skilled page more ownable than a page built with no skill?** Earlier rounds measured craft. This one measures distinctiveness with a swap-the-name blind comparison, because the verdicts so far were "better, just not top tier" and "good but replaceable".

**Short answer: no. On distinctiveness the skill did not beat the baseline in this round. It lost, in all six blind pair comparisons.** It still wins clearly on the mechanical and feel measures. Both statements are true, and the second does not rescue the first.

## What was run

Three briefs, each built once with no skill and once with the skill (same model on both sides; the working copy of the skill at the head of the 0.8.0 branch).

| Brief | Why it is here |
| --- | --- |
| 12, Marrow (contract review) | The plain landing brief from round 5, for a comparison with that round |
| 13, Coreline (drill-core logging) | A product with an obvious native artifact: the strip log |
| 14, Tern (shift scheduling) | A dull product with a weaker artifact: the rota grid |

Baseline agents were told to use no skills and not to read the skill folder. Skilled agents were told to read `SKILL.md`, follow the nine steps, write the three-line concept, scaffold, and run the gate including `swap.mjs`. Nobody could be asked questions. The pages, the concepts and the data are in `evals/results/r6/` (`pages/`, `data.json`, and masked first screens in `shots/`).

## Mechanical and feel numbers

These come from the skill's own instruments (`score.mjs`, `evals/feel.mjs`). The skilled agents ran the same tools and fixed what they found, so these numbers are a floor, not an independent measure, and they favour the skilled arm by construction.

| Brief | Mechanical, baseline | Mechanical, skilled | Feel, baseline | Feel, skilled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Marrow | 75 | 94 | 33 | 77 |
| 13 Coreline | 38 | 90 | 50 | 89 |
| 14 Tern | 17 | 84 | 43 | 77 |
| Mean | 43 | 89 | 42 | 81 |

Round 5 had brief 12 at 83 against 94 mechanical and 31 against 75 feel; this round's skilled numbers match (94 and 77) and the baseline differs (75 and 33), which is the spread between two builds by the same model. The skilled pages had no tells and 0 to 1 contrast problems; the baselines had 1 to 3 kinds of tell, 4 to 19 problems, and two scrolled sideways at some width.

## The swap-the-name blind comparison

Two independent judge agents (fresh, no part in building, each with its own random A/B assignment) saw each pair with the product name masked (`evals/mask.mjs`), the first three desktop screens and a phone first screen, and a one-sentence description of the product. They answered: which page could more easily belong to a different product, which is more memorable, an ownability score from 1 to 5 for each, and which is better designed overall.

| | Baseline | Skilled |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page that could more easily belong to a different product (6 judgements) | 0 | **6** |
| Page remembered a day later (6 judgements) | **6** | 0 |
| Mean ownability score, 1 to 5 (6 judgements) | **4.5** | 3.0 |
| Better designed overall (6 judgements; 1 equal) | **4** | 1 |

The judges' reasons agree with each other. Two things made the skilled pages replaceable:

- **The frame was the same on all three.** Every skilled agent chose `--ground flat --palette sand --hero split --minimal`, so the three skilled pages share a pill nav, a sand rounded hero, a headline and a product card, a 1-2-3 steps block and a dark price band. Judge A2: the three template pages "are interchangeable"; judge A1: "their content is domain-specific, but their frame is interchangeable". Each skilled page scored 3 on ownability for that reason, in every judgement.
- **The baselines carried the product's material through the whole page.** A contract page with a red margin rule and a struck-through headline ("a generic standard" struck, "yours" in green); graph paper with a depth ruler down the edge, a phone and a hatched strip log; weekday columns running behind the headline. The judges named these devices as what they would remember.

A second condition used a third judge on the six first screens with the name masked **and the text blurred**, so that only composition, ground and artwork showed. It was asked which product each page belonged to, which pages looked like one template, and for ownability scores.

- Product identification from the picture alone: 6 of 6 correct, for both arms. The artifact in the hero told the judge the product in every case; this is where the concept step worked, and the baselines did the same thing unprompted.
- Template groups: the judge grouped **all three skilled pages** as one template ("a grain-textured rounded frame on a tan ground, a floating pill nav, a blurred two-line headline, a dark pill CTA, a cream card on the right; only the card contents change") and the other two baseline pages as another hand.
- Ownability on the blurred screens: skilled 4.0 against baseline 3.3 (three pages each). The strip log and the clash-flagged rota carry a page by themselves. The one baseline page that stood out most (graph paper and ruler) was a baseline.

So the concept step does what it was built to do for the hero artwork, and the scaffold undoes it for everything around the artwork.

## What the builders reported

All three skilled agents wrote a concept with a real native artifact (a redlined clause with Accept and Reject, a strip log of one core run, a Monday to Sunday rota with a flagged clash), and all three passed `swap.mjs`. Where the skill got in the way:

- `shoot.mjs --full` showed every scroll-revealed section blank (all three hit it). **Fixed**: it now scrolls the page through first (`revealAll`, tested).
- The split hero's mobile rule is overridden by any later rule with the same selector, so the layout stacked wrongly at 390 px until the agent repeated it. `--minimal` leaves the CSS of the sections it cut. **Documented** in `landing-craft.md`, not changed in the code.
- `check.mjs` reported 1.58:1 contrast on text that sits just below the fold at 390 px (Tern); the agent measured about 7:1 by hand and a copy with the hero text moved inside the viewport passed. **Not investigated or fixed.** A suspected tool bug.
- `craft.mjs` counts a keyboard-focusable scroll region as a control that must change on hover. **Not fixed.**
- The sideways-scroll report named the fixed nav rather than the section that caused the overflow. **Not fixed.**
- No tool drags or presses keys, so interactive artwork cannot be tested by the skill's scripts. **Not addressed.**

## The swap tool did not discriminate, and was changed after the round

On the finished pages, `swap.mjs` first **failed two of the three baselines** (briefs 12 and 14) with "no artwork on the first screen", although the judges rated those pages as the more ownable ones. The cause was a real false negative: the tool only recognised figures, drawings and images as artwork, and these baselines build their product picture from HTML. I then changed the tool to count a framed box of at least 280 by 180 px that is not the hero wrapper, added a fixture and a test, and re-ran. With that change all six pages pass. Read this plainly: the change was made after seeing the result, it fixes a false negative I could name, and the consequence is that **the tool passes every page in this round, so it did not predict which page the judges found replaceable.** It checks that the first screen names the product's own things. It cannot see a shared skeleton, and neither can any single-page check. The written one-line answer at the gate is not a substitute either: all three skilled agents wrote a good one.

## What improved, what did not

- Improved: the craft floor held (84 to 94 mechanical, no tells, feel 77 to 89); the concept step produced real artifacts in the hero; two guidance bugs that agents hit were found and fixed.
- Not improved: whole-page distinctiveness, which is what the owner is asking about. The skilled pages are good and interchangeable.
- Newly visible: the example in `direction.md` ("Ground: flat, because the log is the picture") probably anchored all three agents on flat sand. I cannot prove the anchoring, but three of three is not chance. A line now tells agents the example is an example.

## Limits

- Same model on both sides, and the judges are the same model family as the builders, which may share taste.
- Three briefs, one build per arm per brief. The spread between two builds by one model is unknown; round 5's baseline (83) against this round's (75) on the same brief suggests it is not small.
- Two judges on the visible-text condition and one on the blurred condition. No real users.
- The baseline agents had no time limit and the skilled agents followed a long process; the baselines were quicker (about 1.5 to 7 minutes against about 11), and that has no bearing on the result but is a difference.
- The mask removes the product name from text, labels and the title; it cannot hide a logo shape, and the product facts in the copy still let a judge infer the product.
- `swap.mjs` was adjusted after the round; the mechanical numbers come from instruments the skilled arm used.

## Next steps (proposals, not done)

1. **Vary the skeleton, not only the artwork.** The scaffold gives every page a pill nav, a rounded hero frame, a steps block and a closing panel. Offer real alternatives for the nav, the section order and the closing block, or tell the agent to change three of them and say which.
2. **Carry one material device through the whole page.** The winning baselines used the artifact's own material in the chrome: a margin rule, a ruler edge, weekday columns. Add that to the concept as a fourth line and to the gate: name the device and where it recurs.
3. **Stop defaulting to `--minimal` and the split hero.** Three of three chose them; the result is three identical shells.
4. **A cross-page check.** A page cannot see its siblings, but a project can: compare the skeleton fingerprint (nav shape, section sequence, radii, ground) of the pages in a project and warn when they match.
5. **Round 7**: the same method with the fixes, three builds per arm, new briefs, and a judge from a different model.
