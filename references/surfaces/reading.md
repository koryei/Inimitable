# Surface: reading and documentation (Read)

The visitor wants to understand something. Structure for comprehension first, then make the reading worth staying in.

## Structure

- **One `h1`**, then headings in order; every section has one. Headings state what the section tells you, not a topic label.
- **Navigation on the side** (sections of the doc set) and **on this page** (headings), both quiet. Current location clear.
- **Search** is prominent for sets above a handful of pages.
- **Previous and next** links at the end of a page; a way to give feedback.

## The text

- **Measure 45 to 75 characters** (about 65 ideal), body 16 to 18 px, line height 1.5 to 1.65.
- **Paragraphs** short, one idea each. Lists for parallel items; tables for comparisons.
- **Code blocks** in mono with a copy button, language label, no horizontal page scroll (scroll inside the block), highlighting from the palette (two or three hues).
- **Callouts** (notes, warnings) are few, plain and labelled; not rainbow-coded.
- **Links** are underlined or clearly coloured in running text and distinguishable without colour.
- **Images and diagrams** captioned when needed, alt text always, dark and light variants if themes exist.

## Chrome

- Quiet: neutral ramp, one accent for links and current location.
- No sticky banners, no popups, no modal interruptions while reading.
- Sticky header at most 56 px; an anchor jump leaves room beneath it.

## Reading comfort

- Supports dark and light, 200% zoom, and print.
- Long pages show progress through the on-page navigation, not a progress bar.
- Do not truncate with "read more" when the reader came to read.

## Changelogs and articles

- Newest first, dated, grouped (added, changed, fixed), each entry one plain line with a link when there is more.
- Articles: title, byline and date, reading time only if useful, a short summary at the top.

## Mobile

Navigation in a sheet; the on-page outline collapses to a menu; code scrolls inside its block; tap targets 44 px.

## Bright element

The content itself, set well: measure, scale and rhythm.
